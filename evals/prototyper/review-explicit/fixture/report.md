# Gate-scanning prototype report

Stewards can scan e-tickets at the turnstiles with the handheld scanners that the club already owns, and one gate server per stand can validate scans faster than the busiest turnstiles need. However, the scanners stop admitting anyone whenever the stadium's Wi-Fi drops, so we recommend going ahead for next season only with an offline mode for the scanners.

## Answers

### Can one gate server per stand validate the scans at kick-off?

Proven.

- **Evidence**: The gate server validated 310 scans per second, against a peak of 45 per second at the North Stand's turnstiles during last season's derby.

### Do stewards admit people faster with the scanners than with paper lists?

Stewards were faster with the scanners, and preferred them.

- **Evidence**: Eight stewards admitted 600 club staff and volunteers, who'd agreed to take part, through two turnstiles of the North Stand at a rehearsal on 18 October 2026. Half of them were checked with a scanner, and half against a paper list. The median time per person was 4 seconds with a scanner, and 11 seconds with a list. Seven of the eight stewards said they'd rather use the scanners.

### Can the scanners keep validating tickets when the Wi-Fi drops?

Refuted.

- **Evidence**: The stadium's Wi-Fi dropped twice during the same rehearsal, for 40 and 90 seconds. The scanners rejected every ticket until it came back, and the stewards waved 70 people through without a check.

## What the real project would entail

- An offline mode for the scanners, which keeps a copy of the day's valid tickets, and sends the scans to the gate server once the Wi-Fi is back.
- One gate server per stand, four in all, in each stand's comms room.

## Risks

- **Wi-Fi outages at kick-off**: With 30,000 fans arriving in the hour before kick-off, an outage would leave queues outside every turnstile. We recommend mitigating it with the offline mode, which would take about two weeks to build, since outages happened twice in a single rehearsal.
